Goal set to the present position, so nothing moved. Every motor warmed about 5 C, the fastest in the first minute, whether it drew current or not. The heat comes from the servo electronics with torque enabled at 12 V, not from winding current.

Already protecting the arm

  1. Cool-down failsafe: any motor at 60 C folds the arm to rest, switches torque off, and resumes at 50 C or once cooling levels off. A recording loses only the episode in progress.
  2. Gentle mode: acceleration 40, speed cap about 105 deg/s, 0.4 deg deadband on leader jitter, LeRobot max_relative_target 15, slow catch-up after any big gap.
  3. Guards: blocked joints hold instead of pushing, the gripper eases off under strain or overload, and torque goes off when you exit with the arm folded.

Watch the temperatures and work with the data

Every recorder session logs twice a second: per motor temperature, load, position, goal, current and voltage, plus phase, loop rate and guard events. Rows go to ~/scrub-logs/thermal and to MongoDB scrub.thermal. Anyone with the URI can watch or analyze them:

.venv/bin/python explore/thermal_log.py watch          # live temperatures, any machine
.venv/bin/python explore/thermal_analyze.py --hours 6  # which motor heats, how fast, under what load and phase
.venv/bin/python explore/thermal_log.py snapshot       # every heat-related register on both arms

Run an experiment by changing one thing (fan, a register, the supply voltage), recording 10 minutes of the same routine, and comparing C/min per motor between sessions.
