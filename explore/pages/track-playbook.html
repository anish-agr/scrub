Main loop

One request, end to end. Orange boxes are the product core. Tags on the right name the track each step earns.
Reading the diagram
  1. Voice request via push-to-talk: "Kelly clamp." Community Kitchen: hands-free help for a short-staffed scrub team.
  2. Gemini intent uses function calling locked to the fixed task enum, with keyword fallback. Earns Best Use of Gemini API.
  3. Perceptron detect and palm gate finds the instrument in its tray slot and confirms an open, still palm. Earns Perceptron Robotics (and Perception as alternate).
  4. Fixed task menu means the model can never invent a motion. This is the safety design the Community Kitchen judges will read.
  5. Safety gate is a human-confirm step plus audit log. This is what VEYZL checks and what the findings change.
  6. LeRobot policy runs the pick. Task difficulty and execution score here for Perceptron Robotics.
  7. SO-101 handoff presents the instrument handle-first and releases only when the palm gate agrees.
  8. After the handoff four things fire: Tiger Data log, Backboard preference memory, Photon text to the circulating nurse, and the VEYZL audit on every PR we merge.
04

Pitch angles

One paragraph per panel. Side-track judges can arrive at any time during the 50-minute round, so everyone on the team should know all five.

Community Kitchen judges

Innovation, impact, problem-solving, technical execution

Retained surgical items are a measurable never event, and scrub teams are short-staffed. We built an assistant that does one narrow job safely: pick a named instrument from a fixed tray slot and hand it handle-first into an open palm. The model can only choose from a menu, a palm gate decides when to release, and a human-confirm gate sits in front of every motion. It is a training and research prototype built on props, and we say so on every screen. Impact comes from giving a scrub tech back attention for the patient.

Say: "It can only do eight things, and it can only let go when a hand is there."

Perceptron

Creativity, task difficulty, technical execution; short working-demo video

Perceptron detect with few-shot exemplars finds each prop instrument, and the same models watch the handoff zone for a still, open palm. That vision output gates the LeRobot policy on the provided SO-101. The task is hard because the grasp has to end handle-first at a hand that moves, and we report an N-trial eval with confidence intervals for correct instrument, orientation, latency and retries. We collected our own demonstrations rather than relying on a canned checkpoint.

Show: the eval table, then one live handoff, then one failed trial and what changed.

Photon

Most creative and technically impressive agent; live on a real channel

The robot has a real phone number. When the closing count does not match or an instrument is missing, it texts the circulating nurse with a snapshot, and the nurse can reply YES, NO or CALL. A STOP text latches the arm. The agent lives on a Photon channel, escalates after five minutes of silence, and sends a digest at close. For the voice prize we offer a call-in count readback, which still needs a feasibility check unverified.

Demo: judge texts STOP from their own phone and the arm freezes.

VEYZL

Real audit on a PR, evidence the findings changed the build, short demo

This is a healthcare AI that moves hardware near people, so oversight is the point. We connected the repo, opened a PR, and VEYZL flagged gaps against the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001. We fixed at least two: a human-confirm gate before motion and a tamper-evident audit log, plus a visible training-only disclaimer. The PR shows the check run, and docs/veyzl.md shows before and after.

Show: the PR check, the finding, the commit that closed it.

DubHacks NEXT

Real problem and user, builder energy, iteration, revenue, drive

The buyer is a simulation center or surgical-tech program, which needs no FDA clearance for education and research use. They pay per lab for a handoff trainer and count analytics, with a per-OR subscription for count verification later. We name the clinical path in one line and do not hide it. The weekend shows iteration: v1 handoffs failed on orientation, so we added the palm gate and re-ran the eval. We have talked to a nurse or med student unverified and we will keep building after DubHacks.

Lead with the payer and a price, then the robot.
