# SO-100/SO-101 servo overheating: software, control, and field reports

Scope: software and control causes of STS3215 heating during teleop/record, what others log, and long-run strategies. Researched 2026-10-17. Items marked (unverified) were inferred, came from a secondary/generic page, or could not be confirmed from a primary source. The LeRobot Discord could not be reached by the research tools, so no Discord claims are made.

## 1. Reported problems, root causes, fixes in LeRobot

There is no LeRobot issue titled "overheating". Searching the repo for "overheat" finds only [#441](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/441) (unrelated calibration failure). Heating shows up indirectly as "servo broke", "motor stiff", or "gripper locked".

**Gripper overload (the best-documented cause)**
- [#2819](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2819) (0.4.3): follower gripper became extremely stiff and then errored. Maintainer pkooij: the servo "is overloaded for too long", e.g. the leader gripper is closed fully while an object blocks the follower gripper, so it presses too hard. The reporter later also admitted leaving the robot in a freezing car; the cause was likely mixed.
- [PR #1809](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/1809) (Aug 2025): halved gripper limits "to avoid burnout, which happened more often (probably due to miscalibration and because the gripper doesn't have feedback when the teleoperator closes it fully)", reported by Seeed. In 0.4.4 this is `Max_Torque_Limit=500`, `Protection_Current=250`, `Overload_Torque=25`, applied to the gripper only (verified in the [v0.4.4 so_follower.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.4.4/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py)). The other five joints keep factory protection values.
- [phosphobot #241](https://github.com/phospho-app/phosphobot/issues/241): gripper "went over 70 C" and burned a servo; holding a light object for 30-60 s reached 50 C. Cause: keyboard/gamepad teleop commanded a full close against the object, and the software grip-detect did not work for that input path. Fix in [v0.3.85](https://github.com/phospho-app/phosphobot/releases/tag/v0.3.85): hold-to-close so the servo's torque protection engages. The maintainer also explained the firmware rule: more than 80 percent torque for more than 2 s drops to a 20 percent regime (registers 34/35/36).

**Calibration, hard stops, goal clamping**
- [#3585](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3585) (open): a `Goal_Position` outside calibrated Min/Max is silently clamped by firmware, and (per the reporter) torque can be re-enabled by the write. A commenter saw two of six SO-101 motors "push into the hard stop until they overloaded" and fixed it by setting Min/Max_Position_Limit to 0/4095 while keeping the homing offset.
- [#3586](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3586) and [#3587](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3587) (open): `record_ranges_of_motion` is wrap-blind, and homing offset is set before the sweep, so the encoder wrap point can fall inside the joint's travel. Example: shoulder_lift rest pose maps outside the recorded range. Result is a plausible-looking calibration where the rest or limit pose is out of range. This is the mechanism by which a follower can be commanded into a stop and held there with torque on.
- [#1296](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/1296) ("Magnitude 2114 exceeds 2047"): homing offset is stored sign-magnitude (11 bits), so a half-turn offset above 2047 fails to encode. Workaround in thread: start calibration with all joints at true mid-range. Related fixes: [PR #3373](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3373) (Apr 2026) forces single-turn mode (clears bit 4 of register 0x12) because multi-turn firmware mode gave readings outside 0-4095; [PR #4940](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4940) (Oct 2026) clamps the half-turn offset. v0.4.4 (published 2026-02-27) pre-dates both (verified: no single-turn logic in its feetech.py), so on 0.4.4 the multi-turn/wrap hazard is still open unless firmware was already single-turn.
- [#3942](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3942) (0.5.2): wrong gripper drive_mode/homing leads to the gripper driving to max open and locking up (probably overload cutoff).

**PID and jitter**
- LeRobot sets P=16, I=0, D=32 for all joints ("lower value to avoid shakiness", factory 32). [#3400](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3400): P=16 gives a static-friction dead band (63 vs 26.7 ticks from target for P=16 vs 32). Lower P reduces holding current but increases steady-state error; high D with a noisy goal can raise chatter (inference, unverified for STS3215).
- [#1333](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/1333): wrist_roll instability, thread suggests speed/acceleration tuning; closed stale.
- [#3794](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3794) / [PR #3893](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3893): opt-in EMA (alpha 0.25) plus per-tick delta clip on policy actions; the author notes jitter "wears out servo gears fast". Shows that LeRobot itself does no goal filtering.
- Comms: [#3131](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3131) (0.4.4, 60 fps disconnects, better at 30 fps), [#4282](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4282) (single corrupted Feetech packet kills the loop; SO follower later got `num_read_retries=2`, #4207). Not heat, but a dropped loop can leave a stale goal under torque.

## 2. Control-side mitigations

- **Servo firmware protections** (the real defense; [memory table summary](https://aifitlab-wiki.super.site/feetech-servo-motor-docs/feetech-sts-servo-memory-table-analysis) and [translated datasheet](https://core-electronics.com.au/attachments/uploads/sts3215-smart-servo-datasheet-translated.pdf)): Max_Temperature_Limit default 70 C (torque output shut off above it); Protection_Current in 6.5 mA units (default ~500, 3.25 A); overload 80 percent for 2 s then a 20 percent holding torque; the Unloading_Condition byte (addr 19) enables voltage/encoder/overheat/overcurrent/overload protections; Torque_Limit (addr 48, 0-1000) can cap output. Datasheet rated current is 500-650 mA, stall about 2-2.5 A (column to voltage mapping unverified), so sustained holding above roughly 0.5 A is beyond rating. LeRobot 0.4.4 uses these on the gripper only. Applying a per-joint Torque_Limit (e.g. 60-70 percent on shoulder_lift) is an untested idea here (unverified).
- **Goal filtering**: no LeRobot-native filter. Community EMA+clip is in #3794. Your 0.4 deg deadband and max_relative_target=15 are consistent with this. Note max_relative_target adds a second bus read per loop ("slower fps expected" per the source comment).
- **Acceleration**: 0.4.4 `configure_motors` sets Acceleration=254 and Maximum_Acceleration=254 (fastest, current spikes). Your Acceleration=40 is a legitimate current reducer.
- **Torque off when idle**: LeRobot default `disable_torque_on_disconnect=True`; your override leaves torque on. [#4626](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4626) shows reset code that re-enables leader torque with no torque or velocity cap (`teleop_smooth_move_to`); registers that would cap it are Max_Torque_Limit and Torque_Limit.
- **Gravity compensation / compliance**: nothing in SO-100 stack. The reBot thread ([#4858](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4858), Damiao motors, not Feetech) adds `tau=g(q)` feedforward, goal smoothing with `1-exp(-dt/tau)`, a homing ramp on disconnect, and a three-level thermal monitor (warn, time-to-shutdown estimate, home-and-disconnect). Same pattern as your cool-down. STS3215 has no torque-mode feedforward over the standard position interface (unverified); the practical analog is a mechanical counterbalance spring or parking the arm in a low-gravity-torque pose.
- **Other frameworks**: [phosphobot so100.py](https://github.com/phospho-app/phosphobot/blob/main/phosphobot/phosphobot/hardware/so100.py) exposes temperature, Present_Current (as "torque"), voltage, per-joint PID writes, and a settable Max_Temperature_Limit, with a cached limit; its gripper uses software grip detection. [dora-lerobot](https://github.com/dora-rs/dora-lerobot) feetech-client exposes position, velocity, and current pulled on demand; its so100 config disables torque on startup/shutdown paths (read from code, shallow check). No framework found with an automatic duty-cycle.
- **Generic**: [Arduino forum thread](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/robotic-arm-servos-overheating/524454): an arm that falls when unpowered draws holding current continuously; fixes are counterbalance or stronger servos. The [roboticscenter.ai forum post](https://roboticscenter.ai/forum/posts/so-101-servo-overheating-and-current-limit-settings-for-educators-schools-intermediate) is a discussion prompt with no numbers (unverified/low value). The [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/) has no thermal guidance.

## 3. Telemetry

- Registers (from [LeRobot tables.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.4.4/src/lerobot/motors/feetech/tables.py)): Present_Load (60, 2 B, 0.1 percent PWM duty, sign bit = direction), Present_Voltage (62, 1 B, 0.1 V), Present_Temperature (63, 1 B, C), Present_Current (69, 2 B, 6.5 mA/unit). Load is duty not current; for heating, Present_Current is the better proxy.
- Who logs what: phosphobot reads all of the above on demand; dora-lerobot pulls current; LeRobot's own get_observation reads only Present_Position. A health-check request in [#1319](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/1319) was answered only with "use the Feetech debug software".
- Cost at 1 Mbps (my arithmetic from the Feetech frame format, 10 bit-times per byte; USB-serial latency on macOS not measured, so unverified): a 6-motor sync read of a 2-byte register is about 14 request bytes + 6x8 reply bytes = 62 B, roughly 0.6 ms on the wire. A 1-byte Present_Temperature read is about 56 B, 0.56 ms. A single contiguous block read from 56 to 70 (position through current, 15 B each) is about 140 B, 1.4 ms. Each separate `bus.sync_read(name)` call is its own round trip, so four registers cost four USB transactions. The 30 Hz budget is 33 ms.
- Recommendation: read temperature and current at 1-2 Hz (every 15th-30th loop) with `bus.sync_read("Present_Current", normalize=False, num_retry=2)`, stagger them across ticks, never in the recording hot path more than needed. Block reads need the low-level `_sync_read` (0.4.4 `getData` handles 1/2/4 B only, so you must parse bytes yourself; untested).

## 4. Running for hours

Evidence is thin; this section is mostly engineering inference.
- Duty cycling: STS3215 ratings are low (rated 500-650 mA, load-life test at 1/5 stall torque per the datasheet), so assume holding a loaded pose is a continuous overload for shoulder_lift/elbow. Scheduled rest at a low-torque pose, then torque disabled when the arm is supported, is the only reliably cheap fix. Your 60 C fold / 45 C resume is sound; the hardware cutoff is 70 C, so 60 C leaves margin. Present_Temperature lags winding temperature (unverified), so use rate-of-rise as well as the absolute value.
- Thermal model: first-order, C dT/dt = k I^2 - (T - T_amb)/R. Fit k/C and R*C from a cool-down log and a constant-current hold (cheap, self-calibrating); then predict time-to-60 C from the current EMA, as the reBot monitor does with time-to-shutdown. Not published for STS3215 (unverified).
- Fans: Seeed/LeRobot docs give no fan guidance; STS3215 operating range is listed -20 to 60 C by the datasheet. A small fan on the shoulder/elbow servos is cheap but untested in the literature (unverified).
- Power: [PR #1809](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/1809) and Seeed mention miscalibration as the common cause; also verify supply voltage matches servo variant (7.4 V vs 12 V parts), via Present_Voltage.

## 5. Ranked likely causes in our setup and how to confirm

1. **Gravity holding with torque left on (idle, between episodes, after exit, and at rest pose).** Shoulder_lift/elbow carry the whole arm. Confirm: log Present_Current per joint at rest with torque on; compare to torque off. Temperature slope (C/min) while idle vs active. If idle slope is high, fix by pose or torque-off with support.
2. **Calibration or hard-stop push (#3585/#3586/#3587, no single-turn fix on 0.4.4).** Confirm: print Goal_Position minus Present_Position at rest and at limits; check rest pose lies inside range_min/range_max; check `Min/Max_Position_Limit` registers and whether any joint's Present is outside range; Present_Current spiking at one pose.
3. **Persistent goal error from your own limiters (max_relative_target=15, deadband, stall guard) while the leader and follower differ.** A blocked or lagging follower with a distant leader means continuous high P*error current. Confirm: log |goal - present| and current; look for long stretches above 3-5 deg with current above ~0.6 A.
4. **Gripper stall on object or leader closed past contact.** Confirm: gripper current while closed on an object and at full close; check the 250/500/25 values are actually written (`bus.read`), since you changed configure behavior.
5. **Leader noise driving D=32 chatter / dithering at 30 Hz.** Confirm: histogram of per-tick goal deltas at rest, Present_Current standard deviation at hold, audible buzz; test with an EMA on goals (alpha about 0.25) or stronger deadband and compare temperature slope.
6. **Acceleration/velocity profile (Acceleration=40, Goal_Velocity=1200) causing peaks.** Confirm: peak Present_Current during fast moves vs gentler settings; likely minor compared with 1-3.
7. **Supply/voltage or wrong servo variant.** Confirm: Present_Voltage steady and matching spec.
8. **Read-retry/loop drop leaving stale goals under torque.** Confirm: count retries and loop overruns; low likelihood.

Single best experiment: run the arm idle for 20 minutes in three states (torque off, torque on at rest pose, torque on in typical working pose) while logging Present_Current and Present_Temperature at 1 Hz per joint. That separates causes 1 and 2 from 3-6 in one session.
